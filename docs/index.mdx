---
title: "Kleos"
description: "A personal memory layer for humans and AI agents: a resolved, timestamped record rather than a pile of searchable documents."
---

Kleos turns a person's sources into a **resolved record** — who decided what,
when it changed, why, what it links to, who owes whom — instead of a pile of
retrievable documents. The sensitive parts are encrypted inside a TEE (AWS Nitro
Enclave) attested via NSM, so the operator holds ciphertext. Authorized agents
query it over MCP under scoped grants, and every read is logged.

Identity comes from stacked OAuth signals verified **inside the enclave**, not
from a government ID.

## The bet, and the measurement

Everything here follows from one claim: that a resolved, timestamped record
beats a pile of retrievable documents. That claim is measured rather than
assumed.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
<Card title="Plain RAG answers with all three" icon="circle-question">
Ask "which database did we pick" and plain retrieval returns every past
decision with nothing to say which one holds. Nothing in the text of a
superseded decision says it was superseded.
</Card>
<Card title="Resolution answers with one, and its history" icon="circle-check">
The current claim, the chain that replaced the earlier ones, and the evidence
that moved each decision — with citations.
</Card>
</CardGroup>

| metric | resolved | resolved + history | plain RAG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| recall | 87% | **100%** | 93% |
| cited | 100% | 100% | 0% |
| unmarked stale assertions | 0% | **0%** | 50% |
| forbidden records returned | 33% | 33% | 50% |

The useful part is where resolution *does not* help. The query graph alone
retrieves **less** than the baseline (87% vs 93%) — the gain comes from marking
superseded claims and from the supersession read, not from ranking. And graph
proximity costs precision across projects. See [Evaluation](/guides/evaluation)
for how to run it and why the absolute numbers are not quotable.

## What this is part of

The memory and permissions core of something larger: a voice-first personal
assistant, with Kleos as the layer that remembers and decides who may read what.
Privacy as a structural property rather than a promise — which means the
boundaries are where the design lives.
